Is stock funding right for you?

It usually suits a business that holds stock it can count, value and sell on — and where cash is the thing holding growth back.

  • You have to pay suppliers before your customers pay you
  • Demand is seasonal and you need to stock up ahead of it
  • Suppliers offer discounts for bulk or early payment you can’t currently take
  • You’re winning bigger orders than your working capital can cover
  • Your bank won’t lend against stock, or wants property as security

Common questions

How much can I borrow against my stock?

Lenders typically advance a percentage of the value they place on your eligible stock — often somewhere around 50–70% for finished goods and traded stock. The figure depends on how quickly your stock sells, how easy it would be to resell, and your trading history. Raw materials and work in progress are usually advanced at lower rates, if at all.

Do I need to own property to get stock funding?

No. Stock funding is secured on the stock itself, so you don’t need to offer property as security. Lenders will usually ask for a personal guarantee from the directors.

Can stock finance work alongside invoice finance?

Yes, and for many trading businesses it’s the best combination: stock finance to buy goods, invoice finance to release cash once they’re sold on credit terms. Some lenders offer both as one facility. Read our comparison.

How long does it take?

Indicative terms can often be in place within days of us receiving your information. A full facility usually takes a few weeks, because the lender will want to value or audit your stock first.

What do you charge?
