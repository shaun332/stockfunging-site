Vehicle stocking finance for motor dealers

For independent car, van, motorhome and specialist vehicle dealers. Whether you need your first stocking plan, a bigger limit, or a second line alongside your existing one, we compare providers so you don’t have to.

Why use a broker for a stocking plan?

Most stocking providers only sell their own plan. They’ll tell you what they can offer, not whether someone else would offer more for less. As an independent broker, we look across the market and match your dealership to the provider that suits how you trade.

We’re particularly useful if you’re:

  • A new or start-up dealer with limited trading history or accounts
  • Turned down by a stocking provider, or offered a limit too small to be useful
  • Outgrowing your current plan and need a higher limit or a second facility
  • Dealing in specialist stock — motorhomes and caravans, light commercials, motorcycles, classics or EVs — that mainstream plans are wary of
  • Unhappy with your current terms and want to know what else is out there

How a stocking plan works

  1. You buy a vehicle at auction, in part-exchange or from the trade, and the provider pays the seller.
  2. The vehicle goes on your plan. The provider usually keeps ownership of it until you’ve paid it off.
  3. You pay as you go. Interest is charged on what you’ve drawn, plus any per-vehicle fees.
  4. You settle when it sells. Once the vehicle is sold, you repay what’s owed on it, normally within a few days, and that headroom is free for the next purchase.
  5. Older stock is paid down. If a vehicle hasn’t sold after a set period, you repay part of its value in stages. This is called curtailment.
  6. The provider audits your stock. Expect regular checks that every vehicle on the plan is on your forecourt or accounted for.

What to compare between plans

TermWhat to look for
LimitEnough to hold the stock you need at your busiest time, with room to grow.
InterestHow it’s calculated, and whether it’s fixed or linked to base rate.
Per-vehicle feesCharges for adding each vehicle, which add up quickly at volume.
CurtailmentWhen it starts and how much you repay at each stage. This has a big effect on cash flow with slower-moving stock.
Settlement termsHow many days you have to pay off a vehicle after it’s sold.
Where you can buyWhich auctions and sources the plan covers, and how quickly funds are released.
Audits and securityHow often stock is audited and what guarantees are required.

The cheapest-looking rate isn’t always the cheapest plan. Curtailment and per-vehicle fees can matter more than the headline interest rate, especially if you hold stock for longer. We set the options out side by side so you can see the real difference.

What providers will want to see

  • Your recent accounts or management figures, and bank statements
  • How long you’ve traded and your experience in the motor trade
  • Your typical stock: what you buy, average values and how long it takes to sell
  • Details of any existing stocking plan
  • Your premises, and how vehicles are stored and insured

If your accounts don’t tell the whole story, we explain the rest in the submission. That’s often the difference between a decline and an offer.
